Why do you want to take part in a placement?
Last year, I set my sights on engineering - specifically aerospace engineering - however, I’m still deciding between a degree apprenticeship and traditional university degree. This experience would help me make that choice based on what the work actually consists off and not from what I’ve read online. More over, this programme will give me something my self-directed projects do not have. Exposure to collaborative workflows and professional enviroments. I want to see these methods first hand so I can bring a more structured, professional approach back into my own projects.

How have your qualifications and studies helped you to prepare for a placement?
My A-Levels - consisting of entirely STEM subjects and EPQ - were purpose picked to help achieve my goal of becoming an engineer. My further math and physics studies particularity align with mechanical engineering, and I have chosen to base my EPQ on programming a propulsion simulation to compare with thrust stand and manufacturer data. Through tutoring GCSE Maths and Physics, I’ve learnt that understanding something and explaining it clearly are very different skills. Working on my CREST Gold quad-copter has shown me how quickly decisions in one area ripple into others - changing the motor affects the battery, the frame and the flight controller. I have taught my self about PDS documents, trades study and a failure log.

What other experiences have you had outside of education that have drawn you to the placement programme?
Most candidates can describe an interest in engineering but much fewer can show one. Since the age of 6 - when me and my father built my first computer - I have had access to the expansive knowledge base available on the internet. Fast forward a couple years after near daily playing with Python and C, my father bought me my first soldering station - A Weller WE kit. I became familiar with micro-soldering and controllers such as the ESP32 and Pi Pico. I developed an interest for Cyber security and embarked on my first proper project - Roboscan - a Sub GHz, Rf, NFC and IR multi-tool that taught me common practices and methods regarding network and signal work. Today I'm actively evolving a electric motorcycle, and building a CREST Gold quad-copter from first-principle calculation - built alongside the EPQ. This hands-on work has taught me how to deal with discovering faults and managing time. For example when diagnosing faults on the electric motorcycle I built, I relied on vague error codes and had to hand check each connection via multi-meter. Soon after I had overwhelmed my self and my notebook with values, making it apparent I must develop more rigorous systems for tracking faults. I hope to learn how professional research teams collaborate efficiently to overcome such roadblocks.
